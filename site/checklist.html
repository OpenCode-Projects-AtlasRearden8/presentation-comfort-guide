The Day-Of Checklist

Nothing on this list should be trusted to memory. Print this page, work top to bottom. Two minutes per section. If an item is impossible (no printer in the building, no real door to practice through), do the nearest reasonable version and move on — the goal of this page is that nothing important lives only in your head on the morning of a talk.

The week before (one sitting)

  • ☐ Outline worksheet filled in (one sitting, chapter 1)
  • ☐ Slides built to the ladder (title / agenda / moves / summary) AND every slide passes the chapter-2 finishing list on it: five-word title, one idea, 15-second readability, sourced numbers, zero animations
  • ☐ Speaker notes written, one page per slide (chapter 3)
  • ☐ Rehearsal calendar set: structure pass, full pass, front-door pass

Two days before

  • ☐ Full rehearsal pass done, timing known, recorded once (chapter 4)
  • ☐ [SLOW DOWN] and [SKIP THIS if behind] marks added to notes
  • ☐ Slide-deck PDF exported (the deck can't always be opened on someone else's machine, but everything opens a PDF)
  • ☐ Backup saved: one copy on the machine you will present from, one in email or cloud or USB. If there is a USB drive involved, test it in the actual port if you can. (Really.)
  • ☐ Printed copy of: speaker notes + landing sentence card + day-of checklist + outline worksheet

The night before

  • ☐ No rehearsals. No slide edits. Notes stay as rehearsed. (Chapter 4 is not being unkind here; it is being specific.)
  • ☐ One last look at the checklist only — then close everything.
  • ☐ Doors, tech, and clothes chosen. Lay out the clothes. Small things tonight save micro-decisions in the morning.
  • ☐ Water bottle or plan for water at the lectern. Dry mouth is the unglamorous cause of half of all bad first minutes.
  • ☐ Sleep is for the talk, not slide 7. Go to bed preferring "rested and with a known route" over "polished but gnawed."

The morning of

  • ☐ Eat something small and real. No talk has ever been saved by skipping breakfast, and several have been lost to it.
  • ☐ Front-door pass: first 90 seconds only, out loud, from standing, once. (Not more. Not the rest of the talk. Ninety seconds.) If nerves catch you doing it wrong, do it once correctly and stop.
  • ☐ One look at your landing sentence card. It goes in your pocket or your notes stack.
  • ☐ Drink water. Arrive early enough that you are not the person coming in at the last second; being early gives your nerves a place to stand down.

The technology pass (do it before the room fills)

Ten minutes. Do this even if the meeting setup "should just work":

  • ☐ Your machine charged and plugged in (adapters: have both adapter AND plan B on your person)
  • ☐ Slides open correctly from the actual machine + actual display
  • ☐ Presenter view shows slides to the room and notes only to you
  • ☐ If video call: share the slide window only, not the whole desktop, and confirm the banner and recording status (chapter 3)
  • ☐ Audio tested if you're using a microphone; know where the mute button is on both the podium and the app
  • ☐ PDF backup opens on the display
  • ☐ Remote / clicker tested if one exists; know which key advances and which backs up. If there is no clicker, you are advancing slides from your own machine: decide now where the keyboard lives.

First sixty seconds at the front

You will not remember the ideal posture, the breathing ladder, or any system. Work only this small list:

  • ☐ Notes on the lectern, water at your hand, slide 1 behind you
  • ☐ Look at three friendly faces, one in each section of the room
  • ☐ Say the first sentence slower than feels natural — the room will hear it as calm, and it buys your body the two seconds it needs
  • ☐ [PAUSE] after the sentence: it is allowed to be silent
  • ☐ After the opening: glance at your landing sentence card, and simply know it is there. Do not narrate any of this out loud; this is backstage business.

During the talk — the only three in-slide rules

If your notes are set up right (chapter 3), you need only this:

  • ☐ When a slide changes: say the title as a sentence. (This is your landing pad during any freeze-up, too — chapter 6.)
  • ☐ When you say a number: [SLOW DOWN] and pause a beat after it.
  • ☐ When in doubt about time: [SKIP THIS if we're behind] — the mark exists in your notes; skipping is the plan, not a failure.

Question time

  • ☐ Parking lot instructions ready (chapter 5): "that deserves more than 30 seconds — I'm parking it, you'll see it on the summary slide"
  • ☐ The three moves written on a card: Release ("I don't know"), Bridge ("what I can tell you is..."), Handoff or follow-up ("I'll get you an answer by Friday.")
  • ☐ One follow-through moment: that evening, file the raw notes and run meeting-notes-summarizer-cli on them so the promises you made become a real action-item list (dates included).

After (be kind on purpose)

  • ☐ Name the moment to one person in one sentence: what went well, and what rattled you. (Chapter 6's after-kindness section: one sentence.)
  • ☐ Write the recovery moves you actually used in any freeze — it's evidence for the next version of you.
  • ☐ Post-talk kindness: eat, walk, or go outside before you open the feedback app. The evening voice in your head is not a reviewer.
  • ☐ Send one follow-up email within 24 hours including the summary slide and any parked follow-ups. It puts the parking lot to rest.
  • ☐ One sentence in your notes for the future: what you'd do differently and what you'd keep exactly as is.
